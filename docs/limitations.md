# Scientific limitations

- **Patient overlap in RSNA.** The RSNA `patientId` identifies an image. Through the official RSNA-to-NIH mapping, the 26,684 images belong to 11,452 NIH patients (9,150 / 2,125 / 3,727 per partition), and 3,645 of 5,337 locked-test images (68.3%) come from patients also in development. On the post hoc patient-disjoint test subset (1,598 images, 1,497 patients, prevalence 0.076), a patient-level bootstrap gave gate ROC-AUC 0.9138 [0.8916, 0.9342] versus Swin-B 0.9035 (Δ +0.0103 [+0.0022, +0.0197]). At the frozen RSNA thresholds, however, sensitivity was lower on this subset than on the full locked test (gate 0.595 and Swin-B 0.595, versus 0.851 and 0.853), with specificity 0.934 and 0.924 (121 positives). Patient overlap and the different case mix of this subset cannot be separated, so the full-test sensitivity may be optimistic. No patient-grouped retraining was done.
- **Patient clustering in Kermany.** The 624 Kermany images come from 427 patients, and the test set contains six pairs of byte-identical images (no pair crosses labels). Patient-level resampling changes the external intervals only slightly (see [Results](results.md)).
- **Out-of-fold model selection.** Each historical fold checkpoint was selected on its own held-out fold, so the out-of-fold scores used to fit the fusion rules are not fully independent of model selection. A corrected development analysis (inner validation for checkpoint selection, cross-fitted fusion) gave the same ranking but was not used for the final models.
- **Training variability.** Independent backbone training seeds were not run. The gate-stability checks varied the gate initialization (five seeds) and the training length: on the original out-of-fold scores, training the gate to convergence (L-BFGS) widened its weight range to about 0.09–0.83 but gave cross-fitted ROC-AUC 0.8887, versus 0.8894 for both the 60-step gate and the constant weight (`analysis/gate_convergence_check.py`).
- **Corrected fold ensemble.** The corrected five-fold ensemble is an exploratory retrospective analysis, not a refit of the final models on the full development set. It was scored after both test cohorts had been seen. Its external ranking differs from the reported models: on Kermany the gate reached ROC-AUC 0.9361 versus 0.9327 for Swin-B (Δ +0.0034 [−0.0034, +0.0101]), so the Swin-B advantage in the reported tables depends on the model protocol (see [Results](results.md)).
- **Inference defect fixed after first test scores.** Final-model InceptionV3 inference first ran with torchvision `transform_input=False`, while training and out-of-fold inference used `transform_input=True`. The mismatch was found on RSNA development, calibration and internal-holdout data, after the first RSNA locked-test and Kermany scores had been computed and seen. It was fixed without retraining and without changing the split, checkpoints, gate, configuration or selection rules; temperatures and thresholds were then re-fitted on the RSNA calibration split. All reported results use the corrected inference.
- **Post hoc preprocessing change for Kermany.** KermanyNorm was adopted after the raw-JPEG predictions existed. The change was made to match the RSNA pipeline, used no Kermany labels and lowered the gate's external ROC-AUC (0.9277 to 0.9206); both versions are reported.
- **Timing of the control lock.** The parameters of the matched controls (constant weight, logistic stacking) and their temperatures and thresholds were written to their lock file (23 September 2026) after Kermany predictions from the same backbones already existed. They were fitted only on RSNA development and calibration data, but the lock does not prove that the choice of controls was fixed before any external scores existed.
- **Retrospective local adaptation.** Recalibration on labeled Kermany subsets is a retrospective analysis that uses external labels. It is not part of, and must not be described as, the frozen external evaluation. Draws share evaluation images, so their spread is descriptive.
- **Reuse of reported numbers.** The reported test-set results are descriptive. They should not be reused to select or tune new models and then quoted as independent evidence for those models.
- **Replay fidelity.** The archived KermanyNorm raw scores were reproduced on the GPU server, but byte-level equivalence of input tensors and of the exact executed source snapshot was not fully established.
- **Explainability.** Branch-level attribution does not explain the reliability-gated, calibrated predictor. The original attribution maps were not fully regenerated. Parameter-randomization and baseline-sensitivity checks were pilots (8 and 4 images).
- **Latency.** The interleaved benchmark was a single A100 session with both backbones resident in GPU memory. An earlier benchmark that timed candidates in sequential blocks showed drift across rounds and was replaced.
- **Pretrained weights.** `DEFAULT` torchvision weight symbols resolve per torchvision version (0.25.0 here); the downloaded binaries were not independently re-hashed.
- **Reproduction scope.** Training and GPU inference need the datasets and a GPU. The historical code and the frozen weights are released, but some separately run analyses (corrected development analysis, corrected fold ensemble) are available only as aggregate results; the patient-overlap code is in `analysis/patient_overlap/`, the 500-draw recalibration code in `analysis/recalibration_draws/` and the latency benchmark in `analysis/latency/`.
- **Clinical scope.** No reader study, prospective or multicenter evaluation was done, and no clinical readiness is claimed.
