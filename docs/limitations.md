# Scientific limitations

These points correspond to Section V-A of the paper.

- **Patient-level independence (RSNA).** The RSNA `patientId` identifies an image. Through the RSNA-to-NIH mapping file, the 26,684 images belong to 11,452 NIH patients (9,150 / 2,125 / 3,727 per partition), and 3,645 of 5,337 locked-test images (68.3%) come from patients also in development (3,739, 70.1%, in development or calibration); the out-of-fold folds share patients as well. Resampling patients instead of images leaves the gate-minus-Swin-B ROC-AUC interval nearly unchanged ([+0.0018, +0.0070]). On the patient-disjoint test subset (1,598 images, 1,497 patients, prevalence 0.076), a patient-level bootstrap gave gate ROC-AUC 0.9138 [0.8916, 0.9342] versus Swin-B 0.9035 (Δ +0.0103 [+0.0022, +0.0197]). At the frozen RSNA thresholds, sensitivity on this subset was 0.595 for both models (specificity 0.934 and 0.924; 121 positives), versus 0.851 and 0.853 on the full locked test. Patient overlap and the different case mix of the subset cannot be separated, so the full-test sensitivity may be optimistic. Patient-grouped retraining is future work.
- **Patient clustering (Kermany).** The 624 Kermany images come from 427 children, and the test set contains six pairs of byte-identical images (no pair crosses labels). Patient-level resampling changes the external intervals only slightly (see [Results](results.md)).
- **Out-of-fold model selection.** Each main out-of-fold checkpoint was selected on its own held-out fold, so the out-of-fold scores used to fit the fusion rules are not fully independent of model selection. The nested-selection sensitivity analysis (inner validation for checkpoint selection, cross-fitted fusion) gave the same ranking; it was not used for the final models.
- **Training variability.** Independent backbone training seeds were not run, so the bootstrap intervals condition on fixed models. The gate-stability checks varied the gate initialization (five seeds) and the training length: on the main out-of-fold scores, training the gate to convergence (L-BFGS) widened its weight range to about 0.09–0.83 but gave cross-fitted ROC-AUC 0.8887, versus 0.8894 for both the 60-step gate and the constant weight (`analysis/gate_convergence_check.py`).
- **Nested-selection fold ensemble.** This ensemble is an exploratory sensitivity analysis, not a refit of the final models on the full development set, and it was scored on cohorts that had already been evaluated. On Kermany the gate reached ROC-AUC 0.9361 versus 0.9327 for Swin-B (Δ +0.0034 [−0.0034, +0.0101]), so the Swin-B lead in the main tables depends on how the backbones are obtained (see [Results](results.md)).
- **Settings finalized after initial scores.** The study is retrospective. (i) The parameters of the matched controls (constant weight, logistic stacking) and their temperatures and thresholds were locked (23 September 2026) after Kermany predictions from the same backbones existed; they were fitted only on RSNA development and calibration data. (ii) InceptionV3 inference initially ran with torchvision `transform_input=False`, whereas training and out-of-fold inference used `transform_input=True`; the mismatch was identified on RSNA non-test data and aligned without retraining or changing the split, checkpoints, gate, configuration or selection rules, after which temperatures and thresholds were refitted on the RSNA calibration split. All reported results use the aligned inference. (iii) KermanyNorm (the RSNA percentile scaling applied to the Kermany JPEGs) was adopted under a time-stamped protocol to give both cohorts one preprocessing path; it used no Kermany labels and lowered the gate's external ROC-AUC (0.9277 to 0.9206). Both Kermany versions are reported.
- **Retrospective local adaptation.** Recalibration on labeled Kermany subsets uses external labels and is reported separately from the frozen external evaluation. Draws share evaluation images, so their spread is descriptive.
- **Reuse of reported numbers.** The reported test-set results are descriptive. They should not be reused to select or tune new models and then quoted as independent evidence for those models.
- **Replay fidelity.** The archived KermanyNorm raw scores were reproduced on the GPU server, but byte-level equivalence of input tensors and of the executed source snapshot was not fully established.
- **Explainability.** Branch-level attribution describes each backbone, not the reliability-gated, calibrated predictor. The original attribution maps were not fully regenerated. Parameter-randomization and deletion-baseline checks used small subsets (8 InceptionV3 and 4 Swin-B Kermany images).
- **Latency.** The interleaved benchmark was a single A100 session with both backbones resident in GPU memory. A sequential block design showed drift across rounds, so the interleaved design is the one reported.
- **Pretrained weights.** `DEFAULT` torchvision weight symbols resolve per torchvision version (`IMAGENET1K_V1` for both backbones in torchvision 0.25.0); the downloaded binaries were not independently re-hashed.
- **Reproduction scope.** Training and GPU inference need the datasets and a GPU. The training code and the frozen weights are released; the nested-selection analysis can be re-run from its private OOF scores with `analysis/nested_selection/` (the backbone training for it used server-side modules that are not part of this release); the nested-selection fold ensemble is available only as aggregate results; the patient-overlap code is in `analysis/patient_overlap/`, the 500-draw recalibration code in `analysis/recalibration_draws/` and the latency benchmark in `analysis/latency/`.
- **Scope of conclusions.** RSNA (adult, opacity labels, abnormal negatives) and Kermany (pediatric, pneumonia versus normal) differ jointly in several respects; an adult external cohort with an aligned label definition would strengthen the transport conclusions. No reader, prospective or multicenter study was done, and no clinical readiness is claimed.
